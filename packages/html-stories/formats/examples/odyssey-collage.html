Literary geography · Homer’s Odyssey

A two-week voyage, 10ten years long.

Troy to Ithaca is 565 nautical miles in a straight line, a week or two of Bronze Age sailing. Homer stretches it into ten years, and only a few weeks of them are spent at sea.

Fifteen landfalls, cut from paper. Three rest on excavation. The rest are tradition, argument, or Wonderland.

The time ruler

A week by galley. Ten years by Homer.

Days to sail from Troy to Ithaca, on a scale where each gridline is a longer unit of time. Everyone who has tried it since got home in under two years.

Troy to Ithaca, days under way

Sources: Harry Mount, BOAT International; Tim Severin, The Ulysses Voyage; Hal Roth, We Followed Odysseus.

1
1 · Troy to Ismaros, ThraceDocumented

Twelve ships leave Troy. The first raid costs 72 men.

Odysseus sails from the sacked city with twelve ships and about 720 men. At Ismaros he raids the Cicones, lingers, and loses six men from every ship when they counter-attack.

Both places are excavated: Hisarlik is Troy, and Maroneia in Thrace is the strongest identification in the whole poem.

2
2 · Cape Malea, PeloponneseReal place

A north wind off Cape Malea blows him off the map.

Rounding the cape for home, the fleet is driven past Cythera and drifts for nine days. Stanford called it the last clearly identifiable place in the wanderings: after this, Odysseus leaves geography and enters Wonderland.

Of fifteen landfalls, three stand on excavation. Eleven are tradition or argument.

3
3 · The LaestrygoniansContested

Eleven of twelve ships, lost in one harbour.

Cannibal giants pelt the moored fleet with rocks. Only Odysseus’s ship, tied up outside the harbour mouth, cuts its cable and escapes. The single greatest loss of the voyage happens in a few lines.

Where? Thucydides put the Laestrygonians in Sicily. Severin sailed to Mezapos bay in the Mani. Nobody has found a harbour full of rocks.

4
4 · Aeaea and OgygiaInferred

Eight of the ten years are two islands.

A year with the sorceress Circe. Seven with the nymph Calypso. The monsters, the storms and the drifting fit inside a single sailing season.

The poem gives the years. The months, and the twenty it never accounts for, are our arithmetic.

5
5 · Scylla to ThrinaciaIn the text

720 men leave Troy. One comes home.

Scylla takes six, one for each head. On Thrinacia the crew slaughter the cattle of the Sun, and Zeus sinks the last ship with everyone aboard. Odysseus floats to Calypso on the keel.

The headcount between the losses is reconstructed at about sixty men a ship. The losses themselves are the poem’s own numbers.

6
6 · Ithaca, and the five mapsContested

Five schools, five Mediterraneans.

Every reconstruction agrees until Cape Malea. After it, the ancient geographers keep him around Sicily, Bradford takes him to the western basin, Bérard out to Gibraltar, and Severin never leaves the Greek coast.

The sailor’s reckoning

A fortnight, with a fair wind

Troy to Ithaca down the Aegean and round Cape Malea: 565 nautical miles. Six to fourteen days for a Bronze Age galley. Severin’s replica took three to four months, stopping to argue the route.

The poet’s reckoning

Ten years, mostly ashore

Eight years on two islands. A month with Aeolus. A year’s detour to the Underworld and back. The voyaging itself, nine days adrift included, fits in one season.

Method and caveats

Fact: the fleet numbers, the losses and the stated durations come from the poem. Interpretation: the men aboard between losses are reconstructed at about 60 a ship, and the months are our arithmetic on the poem’s years.

Every landfall after Cape Malea is a tradition or a rival identification, stamped as such; none is treated as a site. The 565 nautical miles are a straight line, not a sailed track. Nobody rowed it in a fortnight.

The collage is cut paper drawn in code. The page carries no photographs.
