The Odyssey, measured

A two-week sail that took ten years

Troy to Ithaca is 273 nautical miles as the crow flies, and about 424 by sea round Cape Malea: a week or two at a galley's pace. Homer makes it last a decade.

Where the decade goes

Eight of those ten years, he isn't sailing at all

Each tower rises by the time Odysseus spends ashore. Two islands of captivity dwarf the voyage: one year with Circe, then seven with Calypso.

The decade, one cell per year

Circe, a year. Calypso, seven. Hatched: weeks at sea, a month with Aeolus, a month storm-bound on Thrinacia, and time the poem leaves unaccounted.

The hinge

Past this cape, the map stops being a map

Rounding Cape Malea, a north wind drives the fleet for nine days. Up to here every landfall can be found on a chart. After it, none can be pinned down.

How far is nine days?
630–900 mi. A galley running before the wind lands in Tunisia: the traditional Djerba.
≈270 mi. A crew fighting the wind drifts about 30 mi a day: Libya, says Tim Severin.
From the coordinates: Malea to Djerba is 717 mi; Malea to the Cyrenaican coast, about 259.

Why wine-dark, and never blue?

17uses of the word in Homer: five in the Iliad, twelve in the Odyssey.

Oxenthe only other thing Homer calls wine-faced.

The nine bands are this story's palette, not a reconstruction of any ancient pigment.

Which Ithaca?

Even the homecoming has two addresses

Modern Ithaki has tradition and Mycenaean-era finds on its side. A rival case puts Homer's Ithaca on Paliki, Kefalonia's western peninsula, 35 km away.

The traditional caseIthaki

Mycenaean-era finds at Aetos; the Cave of the Nymphs matched to Homer's harbour.

The rival casePaliki

Robert Bittlestone, Odysseus Unbound (2005): low and facing west, as Homer describes, and once, he argued, an island.

A live debate · checked 21 October 2026In July 2026 James Diggle and John Underhill argued that Paliki was not an island in the Late Bronze Age, and that Homer never calls Ithaca one. They still place it on Kefalonia. Both cases stay marked contested.

Epilogue

Eratosthenes and the cobbler

Two thousand years of mapmakers have tried anyway. The model holds their best guesses, and marks which ones are only that.

How this model was made

Coastlines are Natural Earth land (world-atlas, 1:50m; 1:10m for the Ionian close-up) in a Mercator projection fitted to 9°W–37°E, 30–46°N. Every distance is a great-circle figure computed from the coordinates of the places named; where the research gave a different number, the computed one is used.

Each pin carries how sure anyone is: excavated (a solid bronze dot), the hinge (a diamond), tradition (a hollow dot) or contested (a dashed ring). Nothing past Cape Malea is marked as more than tradition.

Caveats

  • Fleet and crew numbers are reconstructed: twelve ships at about 60 men each. Homer gives the losses, not a census.
  • Most classicists treat the landfalls after Cape Malea as imaginary; the routes are shown as layers, not answers.
  • The Ithaca and Paliki debate is live (Diggle and Underhill, July 2026).
