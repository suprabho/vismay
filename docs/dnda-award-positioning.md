# Positioning Vismay for the Don Norman Design Award (DNDA26)

*Strategy memo · June 2026 · for Supro*

## Bottom line

DNDA is not a design-quality or innovation award. It rewards **Humanity-Centered Design (HCD+) for societal benefit**, tied to the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and it explicitly excludes "purely technological" work that lacks a direct, evidenced benefit to a specific group of people. Vismay, as it stands today, is a commercial data-storytelling studio and a viz engine — which is precisely the kind of profile the award is built to filter *out* unless there's a genuine societal-benefit story underneath it.

So the real question isn't "how do we write a winning application." It's "is there a defensible, honest angle where Vismay's work serves people — and if not yet, what would we build to create one." My recommendation:

- **Don't chase the Projects (Early-Career) contest in 2026.** It needs a defined community, before/after impact evidence, and community co-design. Vismay doesn't have that today, and forcing it risks landing in exactly the "technological project" bucket the judges reject.
- **Education for HCD+ is a non-starter.** Vismay trains partners to run media IP, not to do humanity-centered societal design.
- **The Organization track is the only realistic path — and even it is a stretch today.** It's recognition (not a contest), it accepts for-profits, and it's permanent. But it requires evidence that the organization genuinely *supports projects that advance societal well-being*, and judges actively screen for "greenwashing" and net harm. A thin, spun version of the current commercial deck would likely be declined.

The strongest move is therefore a two-step play: stand up one real public-interest initiative under Vismay, gather modest evidence over a cycle, and apply on substance — not narrative alone. The 2026 Organization deadline (August 17) gives runway; if the evidence isn't there, DNDA's planned 2027 "Technology for Humanity" category may fit the engine better.

## What DNDA actually rewards (and how I know)

The 2025 laureates make the bar concrete. Projects winners: a care ecosystem for elderly residents in north Singapore, a cookbook co-created with immigrant Bangladeshi women in New York, coffee-farm wastewater treatment in Colombia, community-redesigned learning spaces in Malaysia, frontline healthcare workers in Indonesia, Indigenous treaty work in Canada. Organization winners: Goonj, the Aga Khan Foundation's learning hub, public-service innovation labs, foundations — alongside a few for-profits (Frasers Property, a textiles company) recognized for concrete community programs, not for their core business being profitable.

Three things are non-negotiable across all of them:

1. **A specific group of people whose lives measurably improved** — not a general online audience.
2. **Evidence**, qualitative and quantitative, comparing conditions before and after.
3. **The community is in the room** — co-design (Principle 5), or at minimum deep, repeated work with the target population.

The five HCD+ principles reinforce this: solve root causes, consider the whole ecosystem, take a long-term systems view, test continually with the people it's for, and design *with* the community. The "+" — the societal and community dimension layered onto plain human-centered design — is the entire point, and the guidelines say a project that is "pure HCD" or "pure human factors" will be commended but will not qualify.

## Track-by-track fit

### Projects (Early-Career) — weak fit, don't pursue in 2026

This is the contest with prize money, but it's the wrong shape for Vismay. It requires a defined community, before/after impact evidence collected to minimize bias, the target population on the design team, and team members who are "early-career" (no more than one prior societal project). Vismay's stories inform a broad, self-selected online readership; there is no community whose lived conditions you set out to change and then measured. The guidelines' own "Cautions" section essentially describes this gap — projects that are well-executed but lack implemented community impact should wait.

The *only* way this becomes viable is to carve out a single, genuine community initiative (see "The stronger play" below) and run it as its own project — not to submit the studio or the engine.

### Education for HCD+ — not applicable

This track is narrowly for groups that *train people to do HCD+ societal work*, evidenced by graduates now doing that work as practitioners or instructors. Vismay's partner model trains collaborators to run domain media brands. There are no graduates doing humanity-centered societal design. Skip entirely.

### Organization — best available fit, but a stretch today

This is the right track to aim at, for three reasons: it's open to for-profit companies, it's recognition rather than a head-to-head contest, and it's permanent (re-certified periodically rather than re-applied). The bar is that the organization "truly supports societal good, both in its projects and in the company's guiding principles and spirit."

That last clause is where Vismay is currently thin. The honest gap: the existing deck is a thesis about reclaiming the internet and a business model for building IP — it is not yet a record of supporting projects that advance societal well-being. Judges explicitly weigh whether claimed societal work is real versus PR, and whether the organization is curtailing harm. "We make high-quality data stories" is not, on its own, a societal-benefit case.

## The Organization angle worth building

If you pursue Organization, the narrative has to rest on genuine public-interest substance, not the commercial model. The threads in Vismay's work that can legitimately carry weight, mapped to SDGs:

- **Public-interest information and civic literacy (SDG 16, target 16.10 — public access to information).** The press-freedom story, the "who owns America's debt" explainer, and the prediction-markets piece are the strongest assets here. The anti-algorithmic, human-built, named-authorship mission speaks directly to information integrity — a real, current societal concern.
- **Climate and energy literacy (SDG 13 / SDG 7).** The `/energy-profile` epic — daily IEA ingest plus 33-country OWID energy data, made legible to non-experts — is your most concrete "helps people understand a societal issue" artifact.
- **Quality education / lifelong learning (SDG 4).** Explanatory data journalism that raises public understanding, and potentially Kidzovo if it's framed around equitable access for underserved kids.
- **Decent work and ownership (SDG 8).** The co-ownership model — creators own their brand and community rather than renting reach from platforms — is a defensible "spirit of the organization" point, though secondary.

To make this land rather than read as a stretch, the application would need to show that some of this is **deliberately public-good, not just commercial**: e.g., free and open public access to civic/climate stories, an explicit pro-bono or open-data strand, accessibility of the output, or evidence that the work reached and informed audiences who otherwise lack access to this kind of analysis.

## The honest risk, and the stronger play

**The risk.** If you submit the studio as-is, the most likely judge reaction is: impressive technology and storytelling, but where is the direct benefit to a community, and is the societal framing genuine or retrofitted? That's a decline — and worse, the "greenwashing" lens means a spun application can read badly.

**The stronger play.** Make the substance real before you apply. Concretely, stand up one initiative under Vismay that is unambiguously humanity-centered:

- Pick a public-good vertical (the civic/climate-literacy strand, or Kidzovo framed around an underserved community).
- Work *with* a specific community or partner organization — schools, a local group, an NGO — so Principle 5 is satisfied, not gestured at.
- Run it for a cycle and capture modest before/after evidence: reach among the intended group, comprehension or behavior change, qualitative testimony. DNDA explicitly values honest accounts of what didn't work, so this doesn't have to be polished.

That single initiative could then anchor *either* a credible Organization application (Vismay as the org that supports it) *or*, if the team qualifies as early-career and the community is genuinely co-designing, a Projects entry with real impact evidence. It also future-proofs you for the **2027 "Technology for Humanity"** category DNDA announced, which is explicitly designed for technological projects that indirectly improve lives — arguably the most natural home for the engine itself.

---

## Evidence you'd need to gather

For a serious Organization application:

- A clear statement of which SDG(s) the work advances, and the framework you follow (HCD+ or a named equivalent).
- Specific examples of societal-benefit work, with reach/engagement data and, ideally, evidence of who was informed or helped and how.
- Anything that demonstrates public-good intent beyond commercial return: open access, accessibility, pro-bono work, community partnerships.
- A short, honest account of challenges and limits — judged positively.
- Confirmation that the organization isn't causing offsetting societal/environmental harm.

## Dates and logistics

- **Organization application:** deadline **August 17, 2026** (Pacific). One-pass, no qualifying round. Download the ODT form from the award page to see the exact questions.
- **Projects:** Qualifying deadline **July 15, 2026**; Full **August 17, 2026** — only relevant if you build a genuine community project.
- Recognition, once granted, is permanent subject to periodic re-certification.

I'd lean toward (B), with (A) as a fallback only if you can point to concrete public-good work today.
