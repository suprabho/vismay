The three worlds

Our mark

Our mark borrows from Roger Penrose’s diagram of reality’s three mysteries: the leaps between pattern, mind, and form. None of them makes sense alone.

Vismaya is Sanskrit for wonder: the feeling when something you couldn’t see becomes suddenly, undeniably visible.

We added the z for viz. That’s the job.

01 — Data · Pattern

We start with the dataset, not the story. The pattern has to exist before we decide what it means. We find it, and we refuse to distort it.

02 — Narrative · Meaning

Where stories start

Spin first. Then find the story.

We don’t wait for a topic to arrive. Three randomizers pick one: the Desk for industries, Atlas for countries and cultures, Epics for the places in the great poems. A spin is an assignment, not a story: the research decides whether there is one.

The draw has rules

  • 60% of Desk spins are weighted by heat; the rest are pure chance.
  • No segment twice in 30 days, no combination twice in 90.
  • Heat 85 or more gets back in anyway.
  • Nothing new? Breaking, then Developing, then Evergreen.
  • Atlas spreads across regions; Epics keeps one spin in three philosophical.

The hosting contract

  • One complete HTML file, posted by the agent through our API or MCP tool.
  • Served in a sandbox: scripts run, but with no cookies and no storage.
  • A theme tag tints our header and footer to the story’s palette.
  • A lint checks the page before it goes up, and reports what to fix.
  • Photos: openly licensed first, AI only as illustration, every one credited.
  • Two sites host them: vizmaya.fyi and footshorts.com.

What the world hides

The world is full of important things nobody can see: not because they’re hidden, but because nobody has bothered to make them legible.

The supply chain that keeps your phone alive runs through six countries and a chokepoint most people can’t name. The policy shift that will reshape your industry in five years is sitting in an appendix, unread. The slow variable that explains the fast headline is right there in the dataset, ignored.

What we make

We don’t start with a format. We start with a question: what does this insight actually need to land?

Sometimes it’s a ten-minute scrollytelling piece. Sometimes a single chart with a headline. Sometimes a forty-five-second reel. We’d rather kill a good story than publish a wrong one, and we’d rather make one person think differently than impress ten thousand who scroll past and feel nothing.

Words · data

Shashank Vardhaan

Data storytelling, visual journalism

Shashank on LinkedIn

He started as a journalist who couldn’t stop designing, and a designer who couldn’t stop researching. Nobody hired that combination, so he taught himself: not out of strategy, but because the obsession left no choice.

He believes clarity is an act of generosity: that every number is someone’s lived reality compressed into a digit, and most of the time life gets flattened into a chart no one reads. He exists to give it back its shape, and to refuse to let the distance between what is true and what is understood be someone else’s problem.
